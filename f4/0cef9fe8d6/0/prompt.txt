## Blocking defects

**1. A refused check-in leaves the card contradicting itself — the refusal never re-renders the leg.**
`/home/scottdensmore/Developer/scottdensmore/travel-app/components/ui/CheckInPanel.tsx:71-84` — `router.refresh()` is called only on the success branch (line 84). The validation-failure branch returns at line 77 without it.

I exercised this for real: loaded `/checkin`, then had the airline cancel `UIR-100` in the database (exactly the race the action's re-read-under-lock exists for), then pressed the button. The server correctly refused. The rendered card afterwards:

- badge: **"Check-in open"**
- next step: **"Confirm your seat to check in for this flight."**
- button: still present, **"Check in 2 travellers"**
- alert: **"The airline cancelled this flight. Choose a replacement from your profile, or ask an agent to rebook you."**
- traveller rows: still "Not checked in"

Seen at 390x844 and 1440x900 (`phone-390x844-error-refusal-card.png`, `desktop-1440x900-error-refusal-full.png`). This is the "does it say the true thing" class the brief names: one card asserts check-in is open and simultaneously that the flight is cancelled, and it keeps offering a button that cannot succeed. The likeliest real-world instance is not staff cancellation but the clock: a customer with the page open past T-60min presses the button, is told "Online check-in closed 60 minutes before departure", and the card still says "Check-in open" with a live button — so they press again, and again. Every `CheckInReason` that reaches the failure branch is by definition a server-side state change the page's render is now stale about, so the failure branch wants the same `router.refresh()` the success branch has.

## Moderate

**2. Focus moves to the feedback message with no visible focus indicator on the pointer path.**
`/home/scottdensmore/Developer/scottdensmore/travel-app/app/globals.css:2018` styles `.checkin-feedback:focus-visible`. Measured after a **mouse** click on the button (desktop 1440x900, success; and phone 390x844, error): `document.activeElement` is the feedback `&lt;p&gt;`, but `activeElement.matches(':focus-visible')` is `false` and computed `outline-style` is `none`. Chrome does not treat programmatic focus on a non-interactive element as focus-visible when the last interaction was a pointer. The keyboard path is correct — after Enter, `focusVisible: true` and a 3px `rgb(245,208,254)` ring (`desktop-1440x900-focus-ring-submit.png` shows the button ring; `tablet-768x1024-success-feedback-focused.png` shows the message ring).

User impact: a mouse or touch user's focus silently relocates from the button they pressed to a paragraph with nothing on screen indicating it, so their next Tab starts from an unexpected place. Because the element is only focusable via `tabIndex={-1}`, styling `:focus` instead of `:focus-visible` is safe here — the only spurious ring would be a direct click on the message text itself.

**3. The new "Check In" nav entry renders as "Check I" at 390px.**
`/home/scottdensmore/Developer/scottdensmore/travel-app/components/ui/titlebar.tsx:263-271`. At 390x844 signed in, the nav strip has `overflow-x: auto` with `clientWidth` 358 while the "Check In" link's right edge is at 383 — the final "n" and part of the active-page underline are cut (`phone-390x844-nav-signedin.png`). At 360px the entry is entirely off-screen.

Two things worth separating. This is **not** #193/#256: `documentElement.scrollWidth === clientWidth` at every viewport, so the page body does not scroll horizontally; the nav is an intentionally scrollable strip. And the overflow **mechanism pre-exists** — signed in, the nav `&lt;ul&gt;` was already ~469px against a 358px viewport before this entry (the avatar link and Sign Out were already beyond the edge; signed out, "Sign Up" is clipped on `main` too). What this change does is put the new feature's *only* entry point into the clipped zone, where it reads as a truncated word with no scroll affordance. Keyboard users are unaffected (focus scrolls the strip). Either tighten the titlebar at phone width or file it — but shipping the feature's front door as "Check I" on a phone is worth a decision rather than silence.

## Suggestions

4. **The window pair splits at desktop only.** `auto-fit` with `minmax(min(100%, 13rem), 1fr)` yields 4 columns at 1440 for 5 facts, so "Check-in closes" lands alone on row 2 beneath "Flight", separated from "Check-in opens" (`desktop-1440x900-populated-open.png`). At 768 it gives 3 columns and pairs opens/closes on row 2, which reads distinctly better (`tablet-768x1024-populated-open.png`). Worth pinning the pair together or forcing 5 columns at wide widths.

5. **Present-tense label on a past instant.** An open leg reads `CHECK-IN OPENS — Aug 17, 2026 at 15:15 PDT` while it is Aug 18 and check-in is open. `lib/checkInPolicy.ts` deliberately states `opensAt` even when past, which is right; the *label* is what reads oddly. "Check-in opened" when `reason !== 'NOT_YET_OPEN'` would be truer.

6. **The success state says the same thing twice**: next step "You are checked in. Bring photo identification to the airport." immediately above the alert "Checked in for Mona Airways UIR-100. Bring photo identification to the airport."

7. **A round trip's two legs are separated by an unrelated booking.** Ordering by `departureDate` (`app/checkin/page.tsx:93`) renders "Departing: Seattle → Detroit", then "Departing: Seattle → Tokyo" (a different booking), then "Returning: Detroit → Seattle". Every card is truthful and carries its own confirmation reference, so this is not the #137/#253 defect — but "Departing / Departing / Returning" reads as one sequence, and the second card's reference is the only thing that says otherwise. Grouping by booking, or naming the booking above its legs, would remove the ambiguity.

8. **In-sentence links are distinguished by colour and weight, not underline.** `.checkin-empty a` / `.checkin-link` are `#d8b4fe` bold with `text-decoration: none`; against the surrounding `#e5e7eb` body text the link-to-text contrast is **1.43:1**, well under the 3:1 that WCAG G183 wants when colour is the only cue. Bold weight is a second cue, so it is defensible, but an underline on the two in-sentence cases ("log in", "Book a flight") is cheap insurance. Both links pass text-vs-background contrast comfortably.

9. `role="status"` + `aria-live="polite"` *and* a deliberate focus move may double-announce in some screen readers. The focus move is the more valuable behaviour; if it stays, consider dropping the live region.

10. Uppercase `dt` labels come through Chrome's accessibility tree as the transformed text (`"CHECK-IN OPENS"`, `"CONFIRMATION"`). Consistent with the rest of the app, so noting only.

11. A global `transition: all .25s` animates the new focus ring from `currentColor` white at 0 offset to `#f5d0fe` at 2px offset over 250ms — measured `solid 3px rgb(255,255,255) offset 0px` at t=0 and `rgb(245,208,254) offset 2px` at t=1200ms. Clearly visible throughout; not a defect, but it is why a snapshot taken immediately after focus shows a white ring.

12. Minor comment inaccuracy: `app/globals.css` says the `dt` grey is "5.3:1 on the card"; sampled against the real composited pixels it is **9.06:1**. Harmless in the safe direction, but if any test ever pins the commented figure it would be pinning a wrong number.
