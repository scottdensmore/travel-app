## Blocking

### B1. `calc(100vh - 72px)` / `top: 72px` are wrong: the header is **89px**, and `position: sticky` never engages at all

`app/globals.css:521-524` and `:538-540`. Measured header heights across the sweep: **89px flat from 860 through 1440** (so, answering your specific risk: no, the header does *not* wrap anywhere between 900 and 1440). It wraps to 114.6px at 700–840 and 103px at ≤600 — both below the breakpoint, where `.map` no longer depends on the constant, so that part is harmless. The constant is simply wrong by 17px everywhere it is used.

Two consequences, both measured at 1440×900:

- `.guide` height is 828 and each panel is 828 → `stickySlackPx: 0`. A sticky box cannot move outside its containing block, so **both `position: sticky` declarations are inert.** The old `.guide` had `padding: 40px 20px`, which gave the map 80px of travel; `padding: 0` removed it. So the map does *not* stay put: wheeling over the sidebar scrolled its scrollport to its end (`sbScrollTop` 671 of 671) and then chained to the document, and the map's top went `89 → -149`.
- The panels are 17px taller than the space below the header (`sidebar.bottom = 917` vs `innerH 900`), so 17px of the sidebar's scrollport is below the fold at rest, and the header's `top: 72px` target sits 17px *above* the header's own bottom edge.

The visible result is bad. `desktop-1440x900-journey-02-sidebar-inner-scrolled.png`: the guide's prose scrolls up under the 75%-opaque, `backdrop-filter: blur(20px)` header and is fully legible through it, colliding with the nav. You can read *"Detroit, also known as Motor City, is a city with a rich history and a vibrant cultural scene…"* running straight through **Flight Status**, **Check In**, the notification bell, the avatar and **Sign Out**. Two texts on top of each other, neither readable. That is the acceptance criterion's "overlap", relocated from panel-vs-panel to content-vs-header, and your rect test cannot see it.

### B2. Below 900px the map SVG overflows `.map` and bleeds into the Destinations panel

`app/globals.css:560-564` sets `height: 45vh; min-height: 260px` with no `overflow`, and `.map` is a plain block, so the aspect-locked `&lt;svg&gt;` (`viewBox="0 0 800 500"`, `width: 100%`) keeps its intrinsic height and spills past the box. Measured spill (`svg.bottom - map.bottom`):

| viewport | spill |
| --- | --- |
| 900×900 | **+157.5px** |
| 880×900 | +145 |
| 840×900 | +120 |
| 800×900 | +95 |
| 768×900 | +75 |
| **768×1024** | **+19.2** |
| 700×900 | +32.5 |
| ≤600 | negative (see M5) |

It paints *behind* the sidebar — `elementFromPoint` in the spill band returns `.sticky-sidebar` and `.guide-city-select`, so it does not block clicks — but the sidebar background is only 85% opaque, so it shows straight through. `stacked-900x900-state-map-overflow-into-list.png`: Mexico, Central America, Cuba, the Caribbean and the top of South America are clearly visible ghosting behind the **DESTINATIONS** heading and the New York / Los Angeles / Chicago rows. The new `border-top` divider reads as a seam drawn across the middle of the map rather than a boundary between two panels — which directly answers your question 2 about the divider: the rule is applied (`borderTopWidth: 1px`, `borderLeftWidth: 0`, confirmed) but at 700–900 it does not *function* as a boundary. `tablet-768x1024-state-map-overflow-into-list.png` shows the milder 19px version: a grey wedge of Colombia/Venezuela sitting below the line, above "DESTINATIONS".

Note the spill depends on viewport *height* too, so a short landscape tablet is worse than the 768×1024 you asked me to check.

### B4. The city markers are Tab stop **209**, behind 202 unlabelled focus stops, 176 of them invisible

`react-simple-maps` renders every `&lt;Geography&gt;` as `&lt;path tabindex="0"&gt;` with **no `aria-label` and no `role`**. Measured on `/travelguide`: `geoPaths: 202`, `focusablePaths: 202`, `allFocusable: 246`. Tabbing from the document start, the first city marker is reached at **hop 209** (hops 1–6 are the header links, 7–208 are country paths). Worse, **176 of the 202 paths lie entirely outside the SVG's visible viewport** and are clipped by the SVG viewport — identical count at 1440, 768 and 390, since the crop is viewBox-driven. So a keyboard user gets 176 focus stops on elements that cannot be seen, have no accessible name, and announce nothing.

This is pre-existing (the diff does not touch `Geographies`), but it is the direct answer to your question 5: **you cannot reach the markers by keyboard in any practical sense at any viewport**, and it belongs to the same acceptance criterion this slice closes ("without overlap or inaccessible content"). One incidental effect visible in `phone-390x844-keyboard-country-path-focus.png`: focus landing on a clipped element scrolled the header nav sideways, revealing that "Book Flight" is cut off (see M4).

The ring itself is fine once you get there: `3px solid rgb(192, 132, 252)`, `outline-offset: 2px`, `:focus-visible` matching — so `app/globals.css:768` works. It needs ~250ms to settle because of the global `* { transition: all 0.25s ease }`; my first measurement caught it mid-transition showing the UA white ring, which was my artefact, not a defect. Authoritative evidence is `*-keyboard-marker-focusring-zoom.png` (ignore `*-journey-11-marker-focus.png` and `*-state-keyboard-marker-focus.png`, taken with programmatic focus).

## High — the breakpoint and the framing (your questions 1 and 3rd risk)

### H1. 900 is too low: the row layout is already unusable well above it

`row-901x900-01-rest.png` answers this outright. At 901px the map column is 429×828 and the world map inside it is **429×268 — a postage stamp pinned to the top-left corner above 560px (68%) of empty gradient.** The whole eastern US is a smudge and the markers are 5px dots. It looks broken, not intentional. The empty band by width (`.map` height 828 throughout):

| width | svg height | empty below | % of panel empty |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 901 | 268 | 560 | 68% |
| 1024 | 345 | 483 | 58% |
| 1120 | 405 | 423 | 51% |
| 1280 | 505 | 323 | 39% |
| 1440 | 605 | 223 | 27% |

Only around 1280–1440 does the row read as deliberate. So: 900 is the right *side* to stack on, but the wrong number — the row wants roughly 1100–1200, or `.map` wants to stop being a fixed `100vh − header` box holding a top-aligned aspect-locked SVG (centre it, and/or size the box from the SVG's aspect ratio).

### H2. Desktop 1440: legibly framed, not cropped or stretched — but centred on open ocean

`desktop-1440x900-01-rest.png`. The map fills 968×605 with no distortion (uniform viewBox scale, `preserveAspectRatio` default), no awkward crop of the subject, and 223px of soft-gradient letterbox below which reads acceptably because of `.map`'s radial gradient. So the answer to "legibly framed" is yes at 1440.

"Centred on something sensible" is a no. `projectionConfig={{ scale: 600, center: [-70, 28] }}` (`TravelGuideClient.tsx:134`) puts the centre in the Atlantic off the Bahamas, so all ten seeded cities cluster in the upper-left quadrant and the right ~55% is empty ocean. Los Angeles lands at x=21.3 at 1440 (the circle spans x 15–27, 15px from the viewport edge) and the Californian coastline is cropped off. There is an irony worth knowing: the 216px the old absolute sidebar covered was exactly that empty Atlantic on the map's right — so removing the overlap *exposed* the emptiest part of the frame, and the map looks emptier now than it did broken. Moving the centre west (roughly `[-95, 38]`) and/or raising the scale would make the frame match its content. Not a regression; a presentation follow-up.

### H3. Marker hit targets fail WCAG 2.5.8 at every viewport, badly at phone

`r={5}` in viewBox units scales with the map width. Measured rendered diameters: **12.1px at 1440, 9.6px at 768, 4.875px at 390** (selected marker 19.4 / 15.4 / 7.8). WCAG 2.5.8 AA wants 24×24. I could only click them with a synthetic precise/`force` click — a finger cannot hit a 4.9px dot, so the practical answer to "can you see and click the city markers" at 390 is: see, barely; tap, no. This is an *improvement* on the prior state (the map computed to width 0 at 390, so markers did not exist, and were 3.2px at 768), so it is not a regression — but this slice is the one claiming mobile widths work, so a hit-target floor belongs in scope or in an issue.
